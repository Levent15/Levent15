# Buy Where One Game Won't Void the Return

Drive first to **Sports Basement Berkeley (2727 Milvia St, about 2 miles away, reported open Saturday 10am-7pm, (510) 984-3907)**. It is the only store in range whose written policy takes back **"new or gently used items" "at any time"** for refund, credit or repair, so it is the one place where a return after playing a game is plausibly covered in writing. Try every current Nike Tiempo (Ligera Pro, Maestro Elite, Maestro Academy) in **10.5**, the adidas Copa Pure IV in **10.5 and then 10**, and the Puma King 20 Ultimate in **10.5**. If Berkeley lacks your size, the brand you want picks the next loop. For Nike, go to Sports Basement Bryant St and then **Nike San Francisco (278 Post St)**, which is now the only full-price Nike store in range because Berkeley's Fourth Street Nike shows as closed. Return anything worn there within 30 days, which keeps you covered under either reading of Nike's rules. For adidas or Puma, go to Sports Basement Walnut Creek and then DICK'S Pleasant Hill, where a worn pair gets, at best, an exchange if the manager agrees. Your fit history needs two adjustments. Nike ended the Tiempo Legend line in January 2026, so your Legend 10 Elite survives only as leftover stock, and the Copa Mundial's full-size-down rule does not apply to modern Copas. The kangaroo classics (Copa Mundial, Nike Premier 3) cannot legally be sold in California, so assume no local store has them unless one confirms by phone. Even then, the likeliest seller, Soccer Post, refuses returns of worn cleats. Phone each store before you drive, because several key details below are unverified.

**Sports Basement** comes first because its "Love Your Purchase" policy reads: "If at any time you don't love your purchase, you can bring back new or gently used items for refund, credit or repair (proof of purchase required)" ([Sports Basement](https://www.sportsbasement.com/pages/return-policy)). This report treats one game as "gently used," but no source addresses cleats specifically. The policy also does not say whether you or the store chooses between refund, credit and repair. Two limits apply. A secondary guide says returns must be "perfectly clean," and that muddy items may be refused or charged a cleaning fee (unverified) ([The Return Guide](https://thereturnguide.com/blogs/retail-store-returns/sports-basement-return-policy-guide)). Sports Basement can also suspend the policy for customers whose return rate runs "2-3 times the average" ([Sports Basement](https://www.sportsbasement.com/pages/return-policy)). It price-matches within 30 days with a gift card for the difference ([Sports Basement](https://www.sportsbasement.com/pages/return-policy)), which takes the sting out of paying full price for the policy.

**Nike-owned stores** come second, and one conflict remains unresolved. Two 2026 guides say most items can be returned within 60 days "even if you have reasonably tested them," subject to inspection for "unreasonable, fraudulent or abusive" activity ([storepolicies.com](https://storepolicies.com/nike-return-policy/); [Footricks](https://footricks.com/blog/nike-soccer-cleats-return-policy/)). Other guides describe a split rule: "unworn OR gently worn" items within 30 days, and "unworn and unwashed" only after that ([Rather-Be-Shopping](https://www.rather-be-shopping.com/blog/nike-return-policy/); [The Return Guide](https://thereturnguide.com/blogs/retail-store-returns/nike-return-policy-online)). Nike's own returns page could not be read, so neither version is confirmed. The fix is simple: **return within 30 days**, which falls inside the worn-item window under both readings. Items marked "Final Sale" are excluded ([Nike](https://www.nike.com/help/a/return-exceptions)). An undated forum thread says staff inspect soles and that acceptance varies by store ([NikeTalk](https://niketalk.com/threads/nike-factory-store-return-policy.679480/)). Refunds reportedly go back to the original payment method, and no source says worn returns get store credit only.

**DICK'S** is a fallback, not a plan. Its rules require shoes in "unworn, clean, and resalable condition," and worn cleats follow the same rules as other shoes, with store managers holding "ultimate discretionary authority" ([The Return Guide](https://thereturnguide.com/blogs/retail-store-returns/dicks-shoe-return-policy-guide)). Managers told one writer the decision "comes down to if they can resell the shoes" ([Rather-Be-Shopping](https://www.rather-be-shopping.com/blog/dicks-sporting-goods-return-policy/)). The window conflict matters little in practice. Most 2026 guides say 90 days, and one says DICK'S moved from 60 to 90 days during COVID and kept it ([Krazy Coupon Lady](https://thekrazycouponlady.com/tips/store-hacks/dicks-sporting-goods-return-policy)), but a search of DICK'S own site returned a 60-day figure, possibly from an old mobile page ([DICK'S](https://m.dickssportinggoods.com/s/return-policy?display=returns&subdisplay=mbg)). Plan around 60 days, knowing that a request involving worn cleats does best within days. With a receipt, the refund goes to your original payment. Without one, you get store credit at the lowest selling price ([DICK'S](https://www.dickssportinggoods.com/s/return-policy)).

Everyone else requires unworn cleats. **Soccer Post's** published policy says "Used, worn, or damaged items cannot be returned or exchanged" ([Soccer Post](https://soccerpost.com/pages/returns-and-exchanges)). That text covers online orders, and because each store is locally operated and none posts its in-store practice, ask before you buy. **adidas** requires that items not be "used, worn, or damaged" ([adidas](https://www.adidas.com/us/help/us-returns-refunds/what-is-the-return-policy)). ## The Legend 10 is gone, so size from your Nike 10.5, not your Mundial 9.5

Nike ended the Tiempo Legend line when the **Tiempo Maestro and Tiempo Ligera** went on sale on January 22, 2026 ([Nike](https://about.nike.com/en/newsroom/releases/nike-football-tiempo-maestro-official-images)). The US lineup is the Maestro Elite at about $250, the Maestro Academy at about $90, and the Ligera Pro at about $150 ([Nike](https://www.nike.com/w/tiempo-soccer-shoes-1gdj0z20w7kzy7ok)). Both new models run true to size against Nike's length-based chart ([footballboots.co.uk](https://www.footballboots.co.uk/review-tiempo.html)). Lockhart found the Maestro "slightly long" but tight enough that true to size suits "pretty much everyone," and flagged an "overly wide" heel that pushes the foot forward instead of locking it ([Lockhart](https://lockhartbootblog.com/2026/02/24/nike-tiempo-maestro-elite-review/)). Reviewers call the Maestro snugger through the midfoot than the Legend 10 ([Footy Headlines](https://www.footyheadlines.com/2025/12/nike-tiempo-maestro-vs.html)). The Ligera, by contrast, has "a lot more adjustability in the overall fit" ([Lockhart](https://lockhartbootblog.com/2026/03/04/nike-tiempo-ligera-pro-review/)). Because the Legend 10 Elite fits you perfectly at 10.5, start every Tiempo at 10.5. The Ligera Pro is the likelier match, but that is a hypothesis to test in the store. In the Maestro, check heel lockdown first. The Maestro Academy uses thicker FlyTouch instead of TECHLEATHER ([footballboots.co.uk](https://www.footballboots.co.uk/review-tiempo.html)), so it will not feel like an Elite. If any store still has a **Legend 10 Elite in 10.5**, that is the one pair with no fit risk. Some retailers sell leftover pairs as final-sale clearance at around $149 ([Soccer Village](https://www.soccervillage.com/dv4328-040.html)), so check the tag.

The kangaroo question needs a plain answer. California Penal Code 653o bans selling kangaroo products. The ban has been back in force since January 1, 2016, with fines of $1,000-$5,000 ([Yahoo/LA Times](https://www.yahoo.com/news/california-banned-sale-kangaroo-leather-120048007.html)). Animal-welfare groups have sued California soccer retailers over it ([WWD](https://wwd.com/footwear-news/shoe-industry-news/kangaroo-leather-soccer-wearhouse-animal-wellness-action-puma-nike-adidas-1238799766/)), and some online sellers refuse to ship the Mundial to California ([Pro:Direct US](https://www.prodirectsport.us/products/adidas-copa-mundial-firm-ground-white-black-adult-boots-1018944)). Nike said in 2023 it would stop using kangaroo ([SoccerBible](https://www.soccerbible.com/news/2023/03/nike-to-stop-using-k-leather-for-its-boots/)), and Puma removed it from the King the same year ([PUMA](https://about.puma.com/en/newsroom/news/0-leather-110-king-puma-phases-out-k-leather-innovative-k-better-technology)). Yet the Premier 3 continues in black and white as Nike's last kangaroo boot ([Footy Headlines](https://www.footyheadlines.com/2025/12/exclusive-nike-premier-3-to-become.html)), and Soccer Post's blog promotes the Mundial and the Premier ([Soccer Post](https://soccerpost.com/blogs/news/three-classic-soccer-cleats-you-didnt-know-you-need)). Those facts do not conflict. Soccer Post is a 47-location chain ([Berkeleyside](https://www.berkeleyside.org/2025/01/13/shop-talk-7-eleven-closes-at-san-pablo-and-university-chase-opens-bank-branch-on-gilman-street)), and a national blog post says nothing about what a California store may legally stock. **In-store availability of kangaroo models in California is doubtful; confirm by phone before driving for one.** Even if one turns up, the likeliest seller does not take back worn cleats, so a kangaroo boot and a return after a game are not available together today.

Judge fit on length, width and heel, not on first-hour stiffness. The Copa Pure IV Elite needs 30-45 minutes to settle ([Cleats Hub](https://cleatshub.com/review/adidas-copa-pure-4-elite)), and an aggregator reports the Ligera Pro's collar and heel stiffen for the first 3-5 wears ([ShoesTrace](https://shoestrace.com/nike/tiempo-ligera-pro)). Match the soleplate to your game. FG studs on artificial turf wear the soleplate fast and can peel it from the upper ([Best Buy Soccer](https://www.bestbuysoccer.com/blogs/buyers-guides/fg-vs-ag-vs-sg-cleats-which-soccer-cleat-studs-for-which-surface)), and that kind of damage makes a return look like abuse. If you play on turf, buy the AG version (Maestro Elite AG-Pro, Ligera Pro AG) or an FG/AG model (King 20, Copa Icon 2).

## Five habits keep a played-in pair returnable

**Ask before you pay, with the pair in hand.** Put it plainly: "If I play one game in these and they don't work, will you take them back, as a refund or as store credit?" At Sports Basement, also ask who chooses between refund, credit and repair. At Nike SF, ask whether the worn-item window is 30 or 60 days, and confirm the pair is not marked Final Sale. At DICK'S, ask whether a manager would exchange cleats worn once. Tie the answer to the sale: ask staff to write "wear-test OK" on the receipt, or photograph the posted policy, and note the name of the person who answered.

**Pay by card and keep everything.** Sports Basement requires proof of purchase, and its refunds go to the original payment method ([Sports Basement](https://www.sportsbasement.com/policies/refund-policy)). At DICK'S, the receipt decides whether you get a refund to your card or store credit at the lowest selling price. Keep the box, paper and tags, since resale is what managers weigh.

**Test indoors before you go outside.** Wear the pair at home on carpet, with soccer socks, for 20-30 minutes. Soccer socks are what Sports Basement's fitting guide recommends, and it notes that cleats should fit snugly because they stretch ([Sports Basement](https://www.sportsbasement.com/blogs/news/sports-basements-soccer-cleat-guide)). If the length or heel lock is clearly wrong, return the pair unworn, which every store in this report accepts. Take them outside only once they pass.

**Play on the right surface, then clean immediately.** After the game, knock the mud out of the studs, brush the soleplate, wipe the upper, and let them dry at room temperature. DICK'S managers say they take shoes back when "the insoles do not look used, and they can clean the bottoms" ([Rather-Be-Shopping](https://www.rather-be-shopping.com/blog/dicks-sporting-goods-return-policy/)).

**Return fast, and only once.** Decide within a day or two of the game. Stay under 30 days at Nike and well under 60 at DICK'S. Treat Sports Basement's missing deadline as a courtesy, not a license: a single fit return is what the policy is for, and a pattern of game-test returns is what its abuse clause targets.

## Conclusion

Two of your goals pull in opposite directions. The lenient return policies belong to general retailers, a regional outdoor chain and Nike's own store, and they sell synthetic and calf-leather boots. The kangaroo feel you know from the Mundial is legally off California shelves, and where it does surface, it is sold on no-worn-return terms. So today's real decision is among the Tiempo successors, the Copa Pure IV and the King 20, and Sports Basement's policy works as insurance on one fit experiment, not as a way to sample several boots on the field.

Store closures have changed which option is closest. With Berkeley's Nike store and San Francisco's adidas store both gone, the nearest brand-owned trial policy is now a Bay Bridge trip away, and the nearest specialist has one of the strictest policies in the set. A Berkeley player who wants the freedom to return cleats after a game should shop at the Bay Area chain on Milvia Street first and cross the bridge only for a specific Nike model.
